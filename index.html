Current research

The Fateful Adventures of the Good Soldiers

Loyalty, desertion, and the Czechoslovak Legions, 1914–1920.

Wartime desertion is usually studied in conscript armies, where staying may reflect fear rather than loyalty. The Legions were a volunteer force, formed from emigrants, prisoners of war, and men who had already deserted the Habsburg army — so the same individuals can be watched choosing twice, under different incentives. The question is why men who had already chosen the nation once would leave its army again, and whether nationalism in their home areas before the war kept them loyal. Because enlistment was voluntary, the setting strips out much of the coercion confound and gets closer to what actually sustained commitment.

  • Records
    ~320,000 individual soldier records across two datasets, not previously used in economic research: 132,993 legionnaires and 186,192 fallen Habsburg soldiers
  • Nationalism
    Measured place by place Czech amateur theatre in 7,368 places, 1780–1913, and 11,128 entries in the member lists of the Matice česká and the Matice moravská, digitised from scans the 1910 register of every settlement, with its schools and other institutions, read by language models and checked against hand transcription
  • Geography
    350 districts 158 political districts in Bohemia, Moravia and Austrian Silesia, and 192 district-level units across 23 counties of Upper Hungary 1910 censuses of both halves of the monarchy, joined to birthplaces through settlement crosswalks
  • Status
    In progress Master’s thesis Paper in preparation supervised by Sebastian Ottinger · data construction done and checked, analysis next
